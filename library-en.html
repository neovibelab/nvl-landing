The frames we read
the industry with

The tools Neo Vibe Lab reaches for again and again when it analyzes the entertainment and content industry. Six years of newsletters and research, with the timely cases stripped away, leaving only the readings that keep working as the era changes.

We treat entertainment as a reference other industries consult, and music as the place where the next change shows up first. So under every frame we wrote "what another industry can take". The same structure repeats in every business that needs fans. The source newsletters linked below are in Korean.

Analytical Frames

Twelve analysis frames

Each frame is one line of definition and one line of application. Where a published piece backs it, the link is attached (Korean).

Fandom · Models
01

A fandom optimization model, not a genre

K-pop is not a type of music but a methodology for creating fans. This is why every attempt to define "what K is" through sound alone has failed.

What another industry can takeIf explaining your business through a category definition gets you stuck, what needs defining may not be the product but the process of creating customers.

02

The five stages of fandom business

Fans are divided into layers: CONTACT → DIVE → PLAY → LOVE → DEVOTION, and each layer wants something different. The premise is treating fans as colleagues, not customers.

What another industry can takeAn organization that treats membership and community as a single group is likely spending benefits meant for the deepest layer on the shallowest one.

03

The agency, artist, and fan triangle, and the 7 years

The interests of the three parties never align from the start. The 7-year term of the standard contract periodically compresses that misalignment and lets it burst.

What another industry can takeThe contract expiration date looks like the cause of conflict, but it is actually the point where conflicts of interest, normally scattered, converge at a single moment. The same dynamic exists across dealer, franchise, and exclusive-contract structures.

04

The paradox of the content business

Content that does not make money is a necessary condition for the business. If you cut unprofitable activities, the basis for profitable activities disappears with them.

What another industry can takeThis is why organizations that restructure divisions based on profit and loss alone find themselves, years later, wondering why no one comes to them anymore. It is a matter of density, not scale.

Source · 콘텐츠 비즈니스의 역설

Industry structure · Rights
05

The 7-stage IP business pipeline

Creation → Rights → Primary commercialization → Media mix → Licensing → Experience → Financialization. Having IP is not about owning content but about owning the authority to connect. Korea is world-class at the first three stages but leaves the latter four empty.

What another industry can takeThe ability to make things well and a structure that earns over the long term are different problems. Use this as a diagnostic tool to ask at which stage of the pipeline your own company is stuck.

Source · 한스 짐머는 어떻게 재능을 IP 비즈니스로 확장했을까

06

The assetization of copyright

This is the process by which music catalogs became financial products. As master rights and publishing rights split, and hedge funds and private equity entered as buyers, creative works became assets that generate income without the owner having to do anything.

What another industry can takeOrganizing intangible assets into a sellable form is itself valuation. Brands, recipes, patents, and data all follow the same path.

Source · 마이클 잭슨과 ‘저작권의 자산화’

07

If stage 2 collapses, stage 5 does not exist

If rights management is weak, licensing cannot function in principle. This is why K-pop is closer to a management business than a music business: the rights to the music largely sit outside the company.

What another industry can takeBefore worrying about distribution and monetization, check whether the rights ledger is in order. If the earlier stage is empty, designing the later stages will not make them work.

08

The 3-stage creator growth model

Discovery of talent → Utilization of resources → Assetization of talent. The condition for moving to stage 3 is not scale but a structure in which one directly owns and manages one's rights.

What another industry can takeFreelancers and solo business owners often get stuck at stage 2 not because of a lack of skill but because someone else holds the rights to their output.

Source · Woojin Cha, Selling a Point of View (2025), Part 5

Markets · Pricing
09

The global asymmetry of K-pop

There is no single "global." The market where albums sell and the market where streaming runs are different, and K-pop's position varies completely from market to market. This asymmetry is what the phrase "succeeded globally" conceals.

What another industry can takeThe moment you aggregate overseas performance into a single metric, you lose sight of which market the money is actually coming from.

Source · 케이팝은 정말 ‘글로벌 음악’이 된 걸까?

10

The economics of live ticket pricing

Ticket prices are set not by cost but by the ceiling the market can bear. The resale price is the ruler that measures that ceiling, and dynamic pricing is the mechanism by which the seller recovers the gap between the original price and that ceiling.

What another industry can takeFor products where demand far exceeds supply, pricing is not a matter of cost calculation but a search for the upper limit of willingness to pay. If someone else is capturing that upper limit, it is a structural design problem.

Source · 공연 티켓값은 왜 계속 오를까?

11

The miracle of the mid-size players: conditions for breakthrough

The success of non-mainstream agencies is not luck but a repeating structure. The media environment determines the type of miracle, securing early fans determines the start, and the music engine determines sustainability.

What another industry can takeBreakthroughs by resource-poor players happen in the zone that large players do not bother with. Finding that zone is where strategy begins.

Source · Report: How small labels make miracles · ‘거제 야호’의 법칙

12

Authenticity in the age of technical reproduction

What fans reject is not the fact that "AI was used" but the sense that traces of human labor have been erased. So the standard of judgment becomes not who made it but what and how it was delivered.

What another industry can takeTreating AI disclosure as a binary of hide or reveal is a losing approach. Specifying exactly where a human remains involved is what actually works.

Source · AI 아티스트는 정말로 팬을 모을 수 없을까

Theory Index

Five citable frames

Frames quoted as they are in talks and reports, with the definition and the key passage.

The connected structure of content, community, and commerce (3C)

The future of the content business lies in a structure where content, community, and commerce are not separated but mediate one another.

Community becomes more important at the point where product and business separate. Now, in nearly every field, not just music and content but manufacturing and finance alike, almost everyone wants community and fandom. What matters is not scale but density.

콘텐츠 비즈니스의 역설 (2022.12)

The essential nature of fans

A fan is not an ordinary consumer but a special one. They are moved by identity, belonging, and cultural code, and cannot be reached through price, function, or rationality.

Don't stop at wanting fans. Find and focus on what kind of fans you want. Because the mass media era has ended, paradoxically a single fan or customer becomes important. In this structure, experience matters more than message, and substance matters more than image.

단 한 명의 팬이 중요하다 (2022.07)

The next-generation music business model: interaction and engagement

After streaming, the core of the music industry is not listening volume but the depth of interaction and engagement.

The core is interaction. Before long, the influence and performance of music may come to be measured not by album sales or play counts but by user engagement. Going forward, entertainment across every domain will likely be evaluated by how much it can raise the value of this interaction.

내일의 음악 산업: 인터랙션과 인게이지먼트 (2024.06)

Artist leadership in the age of AI

In the age of AI, artists must function not as mere creators but as leaders who integrate fandom, technology, and industry. This applies to both indie artists and K-pop.

What K-pop artists need most is not "good character" but "leadership." Fandom is maintained and expanded only when the artist actively engages with fans, thinking and growing together with them. And this kind of leadership is not limited to K-pop.

AI 시대의 아티스트에게 리더십이 꼭 필요한 이유 (2025.02)

The post-streaming AI agent structure

After streaming, the music industry will be reorganized into a structure where AI agents become the central mediators of curation, discovery, and matching.

The core of the problem lies in the clash between product and process. Existing streaming infrastructure treats music as a finished "product," but generative AI treats music as a "process" that is repeatedly revised through interaction. The solution is not to control AI, but to build an underlying system in which the value AI generates is transparently returned to the original creator.
